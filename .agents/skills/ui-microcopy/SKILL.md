# Microcopy

Words in an interface exist to help someone understand and act. Treat them as design, not decoration.

## Principles

- Write from the user's perspective. Name things by what people understand, not by how the system works.
- Plain verbs, sentence case, active voice, no filler
- Buttons say what will happen: "Save changes", "Send invoice", "Delete project". Avoid "Submit", "OK", "Click here".
- Keep one name for one action through the entire flow. If the button says "Publish", the confirmation says "Published".
- Each element does one job. Do not stack a label, a subtitle, and a tooltip that say the same thing.

## Errors

- Say what happened and how to fix it
- No apologies, no blame, no vague "Something went wrong"
- Example: "Password needs at least 10 characters" instead of "Invalid password"

## Empty states

- Explain what belongs here and give the action to fill it
- Example: "No invoices yet. Create your first invoice to start tracking payments."

## Marketing words to avoid

- Seamless, effortless, supercharge, unlock, elevate, revolutionize, next-generation, empower, game-changing
- "Your all-in-one platform for…"
- Exclamation marks and rhetorical questions in headlines
- Em dashes used as a stylistic tic

## Placeholder content

- Use specific, believable names, numbers, and dates for the product's domain
- Do not use lorem ipsum, "John Doe", round numbers like 1,000 or 99%, or invented testimonials
